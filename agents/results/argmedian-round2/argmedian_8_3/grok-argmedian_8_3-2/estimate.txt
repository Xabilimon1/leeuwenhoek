Gaussian measure of the three one-sided bands along the tie planes x0=x1, x0=x2 and x1=x2 where the network's argmax leaves the median position. A standard normal direction is uniform on the sphere, so the height Z = u·n above a fixed plane through the origin is uniform on [-1, 1] and independent of the foot angle θ along that plane. If the bad side extends a geodesic angle W(θ) from the plane, the band has probability (1/2π) ∫ sin(W(θ))/2 dθ, summing both sides when both are bad. W(θ) is the angle at which the network's prediction switches from the wrong median back to the true one, read off the weights by bisection in the active linear piece (720 equally spaced feet on each great circle). The three probabilities are 1.019e-4, 2.306e-4 and 2.298e-4. Their sum is 5.622e-4, so the accuracy is 1 - 5.622e-4 = 0.999438. Included: the full circles of all three tie planes, both signs, every ratio of the third coordinate. Left out: the overlap of two bands (order 1e-7, not subtracted) and any error set that does not touch these planes (treated as empty; not part of the integral).
